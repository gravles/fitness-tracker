## Executive Summary

The app is already stronger than most consumer fitness apps: it combines a frictionless daily log, AI-powered food and workout entry, gamification, and a real coaching layer. But it's working at the level of *data capture and encouragement*. The gap between "good tracking app" and "genuinely transformative personal health tool" is **intelligence**—taking all that captured data and turning it into specific, personal, actionable guidance that actually changes behaviour.

This document proposes six major feature pillars, each with full specifications, rationale, data model changes, and implementation notes. They are ordered by strategic impact, not implementation effort.

## Pillar 1 — Correlation Engine & Insight Feed

### The Problem

The app captures sleep quality, energy level, stress, movement, protein, alcohol, and more every day. But right now that data just lives in charts. The user has to mentally connect the dots themselves: *"Did I sleep badly because I drank last night? Does my energy crash on days I skip breakfast? Do I perform better when I train in the morning?"*

A correlation engine answers these questions automatically.

### What It Does

**Daily Insight Card** (on the dashboard, below Smart Coach)  
A single, specific, data-backed insight that refreshes weekly. Examples:
- *"Your energy is 34% higher on days you hit your protein goal. You've hit it 4 of the last 7 days."*
- *"You sleep an average of 0.8 points worse after 2+ alcohol drinks. You've had drinks 3 nights this week."*
- *"Your best workout days follow nights with 7+ hours of sleep. Tonight's your chance to set that up."*

**Weekly Pattern Report** (replaces/augments AI Weekly Analysis)  
Instead of generic encouragement, the weekly modal shows 3–5 *discovered correlations* from the user's own data, ranked by statistical strength. Presented as cards with a mini chart.

**"Why do I feel this way?" Quick Ask**  
A button on the dashboard that lets users type *"Why was my energy so low on Tuesday?"* and gets an AI answer grounded in their actual data from that day and the 48 hours prior.

### Data Requirements

No new tables needed. The correlation engine runs as a server-side function against existing `daily_logs`. A new `insights_cache` table stores computed correlations so they don't regenerate on every page load.

```sql
CREATE TABLE insights_cache (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  insight_type text NOT NULL,  -- 'correlation', 'trend', 'anomaly'
  insight_key text NOT NULL,   -- e.g. 'alcohol_vs_sleep'
  payload jsonb NOT NULL,      -- the full insight object
  generated_at timestamptz DEFAULT now(),
  valid_until timestamptz NOT NULL,
  UNIQUE(user_id, insight_key)
);
```

### How It Works Technically

1. A Vercel cron job runs nightly (daily, within Hobby plan limits) calling `/api/insights/generate`
2. For each user with >14 days of data, it fetches all daily logs and runs a lightweight Pearson correlation across key variable pairs:
   - `alcohol_drinks` ↔ `sleep_quality`
   - `protein_grams ≥ target` ↔ `energy_level`
   - `movement_completed` ↔ `motivation_level` (next day)
   - `sleep_quality` ↔ `movement_duration` (next day)
   - `stress_level` ↔ `alcohol_drinks`
   - `calories` ↔ `energy_level`
3. Correlations above r=0.3 (moderate) get stored in `insights_cache` with natural-language descriptions generated by Claude Haiku
4. The dashboard fetches from `insights_cache` — zero latency, no AI call on page load

### The "Why" Quick Ask — AI Implementation

```
System: You are a personal health analyst. The user's data for {date} and the 48 hours prior is:
[structured JSON of relevant log fields]

Correlations we've found in their historical data:
[top 3 from insights_cache]

Answer the user's question using only their data. Be specific with numbers. Max 3 sentences.
```

### UI Sketch

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  📊 This Week's Pattern                              │
│                                                      │
│  Your energy is 38% higher on days you hit your     │
│  protein goal (tracked across 47 days of data).     │
│                                                      │
│  You've hit it 5/7 days this week. ↑ Good week.    │

```

### Why This Matters

This is the feature that makes users feel *understood* rather than just *tracked*. It's the difference between a logbook and a coach. Every other feature in the app becomes more valuable once users understand the "why" behind their data.

## Pillar 2 — Intelligent Nutrition Planning

### The Problem

The app is excellent at logging food *after* you eat it. But most nutrition failure happens *before* — users don't know what to eat, default to whatever is convenient, and then feel guilty logging it. Closing the loop from tracking → planning would be a step-change in nutritional outcomes.

### What It Does

**Weekly Meal Planner**  
A simple Monday–Sunday grid where each cell is a meal (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack). Users can:
- Fill manually
- Ask AI to suggest meals for the week based on their protein target, calorie target, food preferences (derived from Favourites), and any constraints ("I don't eat red meat", "I'm on a budget")
- One-tap to add a planned meal to that day's log when they eat it

**Smart Grocery List**  
From a filled meal plan, generate a consolidated grocery list grouped by category (Protein, Produce, Dairy, etc.) with quantities. Exportable as plain text or shareable link.

**Meal Templates / Saved Meals**  
Currently `favorite_foods` stores individual ingredients. Add `saved_meals` — a collection of food items grouped as a meal (e.g. "Post-workout shake": 2 scoops protein powder + 1 banana + 200ml oat milk = 450 cal / 45g protein). One tap logs the whole meal.

**Macro Cycling Recommendations**  
AI suggests: eat more carbs on workout days, eat at maintenance or slight deficit on rest days. Shows a weekly macro plan overlay on the calendar.

### New Data Model

```sql
-- Meal plans
CREATE TABLE meal_plans (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  week_start date NOT NULL,           -- Monday of the week
  meals jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}',  -- { "2026-05-20_breakfast": [...food_items] }
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now(),
  UNIQUE(user_id, week_start)
);

-- Saved meal bundles
CREATE TABLE saved_meals (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  name text NOT NULL,
  food_items jsonb NOT NULL,  -- same structure as daily_logs.food_items
  total_calories int,
  total_protein int,
  total_carbs int,
  total_fat int,
  tags text[],               -- ['breakfast', 'post-workout', 'quick']
  use_count int DEFAULT 0,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);
```

### AI Meal Planning Prompt Strategy

```
System: You are a sports nutritionist. Generate a 7-day meal plan for a user with:
- Daily protein target: {target_protein}g
- Daily calorie target: {target_calories} kcal  
- Favourite foods: {top_10_favorites_by_frequency}
- Dietary notes: {user_provided_constraints}
- Training days this week: {scheduled_workout_dates}

Rules:
- Workout days: +15% carbs, hit protein target exactly
- Rest days: -10% calories, keep protein target
- Use favourite foods where possible for compliance
- Return JSON: { "2026-05-20": { "breakfast": [{name, calories, protein, carbs, fat}], "lunch": [...], "dinner": [...] } }
```

### New Routes

- `GET /api/nutrition/meal-plan?week=2026-05-20` — fetch or generate
- `POST /api/nutrition/meal-plan` — save plan
- `POST /api/nutrition/grocery-list` — generate from plan
- `POST /api/nutrition/saved-meals` — save a meal bundle
- `GET /api/nutrition/saved-meals` — fetch user's saved meals

### New Page: `/nutrition`

A dedicated Nutrition section in the bottom nav (replacing or complementing the current Log button). Three tabs:
1. **Today** — current day's log summary with macro rings (moved from DailyGoalTracker)
2. **Plan** — weekly meal planner grid
3. **Library** — saved meals + favourite foods

### Why This Matters

Logging is reactive. Planning is proactive. Users who plan their meals have 3× better adherence to macro targets. This feature takes the app from "what did I eat?" to "what should I eat?" — a fundamentally different and more valuable question.

## Pillar 3 — Periodisation & Progressive Overload Engine

### The Problem

The workout tracker records every exercise, set, weight, and rep. But it doesn't *do anything* with that data except show a history. A user who has been doing 3×10 bench press at 60kg for 8 weeks doesn't get any signal that they should progress. Progressive overload — consistently adding load or volume over time — is the single most important principle in strength training, and right now the app ignores it.

### What It Does

**Progressive Overload Alerts**  
When a user starts a workout and loads an exercise, show their last session's volume and a suggestion:
- *"Last time: 3×10 @ 60kg. Try 3×10 @ 62.5kg today, or 4×10 @ 60kg."*
- Calculation: if last session's all sets were completed with good form (all checked), suggest +2.5kg or +1 rep

**Volume Tracking Dashboard**  
A "Gains" tab view showing weekly volume per muscle group (sets × reps × weight), plotted over time. Flags muscle groups that haven't been trained in 7+ days. Based on `workout_exercises` and `workout_sets` with a hardcoded muscle-group mapping per exercise.

**12-Week AI Training Programs**  
A full periodisation plan generated by Claude:
- User picks goal: Strength / Hypertrophy / Endurance / Athletic
- AI generates a 12-week programme with progressive weekly volume and intensity targets, broken into phases (accumulation → intensification → deload)
- Each week auto-populates scheduled workouts with specific targets (sets/reps/load as % of estimated 1RM)
- Programme stored in new `training_programs` table; progress tracked week by week

**Estimated 1RM Tracking**  
For each exercise, calculate estimated 1RM using the Epley formula: `weight × (1 + reps/30)`. Track this over time and plot it. Show personal records prominently.

**Deload Detection**  
After 4 consecutive weeks of training, suggest a deload week: reduced volume (50%), same movements. Flag this in the schedule view.

### New Data Model

```sql
-- Training programs
CREATE TABLE training_programs (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  name text NOT NULL,
  goal text NOT NULL,          -- 'strength', 'hypertrophy', 'endurance', 'athletic'
  duration_weeks int NOT NULL,
  phases jsonb NOT NULL,       -- array of phase objects with week ranges
  weeks jsonb NOT NULL,        -- full week-by-week plan
  is_active boolean DEFAULT false,
  current_week int DEFAULT 1,
  started_at date,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);

-- Exercise 1RM history (calculated, not user-entered)
CREATE TABLE exercise_records (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  exercise_name text NOT NULL,
  estimated_1rm numeric NOT NULL,
  actual_weight numeric NOT NULL,
  actual_reps int NOT NULL,
  recorded_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);
```

### Progressive Overload Algorithm

```typescript
function suggestNextSession(
  exerciseName: string,
  history: WorkoutSet[][],  // last 3 sessions
): { weight: number; sets: number; reps: number; reason: string } {
  const lastSession = history[0];
  const allCompleted = lastSession.every(s => s.completed);
  const avgReps = average(lastSession.map(s => s.reps));
  const weight = lastSession[0].weight;
  
  if (allCompleted && avgReps >= targetReps) {
    // Progress load
    return { weight: weight + 2.5, sets: lastSession.length, reps: targetReps, reason: 'All sets completed — time to add weight' };
  } else if (allCompleted && avgReps < targetReps) {
    // Add a set instead
    return { weight, sets: lastSession.length + 1, reps: targetReps, reason: 'Good completion — adding a set' };
  } else {
    // Maintain
    return { weight, sets: lastSession.length, reps: targetReps, reason: 'Aim to complete all sets before progressing' };
  }
}
```

### Why This Matters

Most fitness apps show you what you lifted. Almost none tell you what to lift next. This feature is the most direct path from "tracking" to "results" for strength-focused users. It's also highly defensible — it requires your historical data to be useful, creating strong lock-in.

## Pillar 4 — Recovery & Readiness Score

### The Problem

Training hard when you're under-recovered leads to injury, plateau, and burnout. But most people have no idea how recovered they actually are. They either train by schedule ("it's Monday, it's chest day") or by feel (which is unreliable under stress or poor sleep). A readiness score gives users a single daily number that synthesises all the signals already being tracked.

### What It Does

**Daily Readiness Score (0–100)**  
Calculated each morning from last night's log and recent history. Shown prominently on the dashboard, colour-coded:
- 80–100 (green): *"Peak — great day to train hard"*
- 60–79 (amber): *"Ready — normal training, listen to your body"*
- 40–59 (orange): *"Moderate — consider a lighter session"*
- 0–39 (red): *"Low — rest or active recovery only"*

**Score Algorithm (no API call, runs client-side)**

| Signal | Weight | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep quality (last night) | 25% | 5/5 = 100, 1/5 = 0 |
| Stress level (yesterday) | 15% | Inverted: 1/5 stress = 100, 5/5 = 0 |
| Energy level (yesterday) | 20% | 5/5 = 100 |
| Days since last rest day | 15% | 0–2 = 100, 3–4 = 60, 5+ = 20 |
| Alcohol (last 48h) | 15% | 0 drinks = 100, 1 = 75, 2 = 50, 3+ = 20 |
| Workout volume yesterday | 10% | Rest = 100, Light = 80, Moderate = 60, Hard = 30 |

**AI Readiness Explanation**  
Tapping the readiness card opens a bottom sheet with a Claude-generated explanation: *"Your readiness is lower than usual today. You slept 2/5 last night and had 3 drinks yesterday evening. Your body is in recovery mode — a walk or mobility session would serve you better than heavy lifting."*

This explanation is generated once per day and cached.

**HRV Integration (Phase 2)**  
If the user has an Apple Watch, Oura Ring, or Garmin, pull HRV data via Apple Health or the device API and weight it heavily in the readiness score. HRV is the gold standard for recovery measurement. This requires the Apple Health integration (Pillar 6).

**Training Recommendation**  
Below the readiness score, show what type of session is recommended today:
- **Score 80+**: suggested workout from their training program at full intensity
- **Score 60–79**: same workout, reduce volume by 10–15%
- **Score 40–59**: active recovery — a walk, yoga, or mobility session from a built-in library
- **Score <40**: rest — a breathing exercise or journaling prompt

### New Data Model

```sql
CREATE TABLE readiness_scores (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  date date NOT NULL,
  score int NOT NULL,          -- 0-100
  components jsonb NOT NULL,   -- individual signal scores for transparency
  ai_explanation text,
  recommendation text,
  UNIQUE(user_id, date)
);
```

### Why This Matters

This is the app's most powerful daily hook. Instead of the user having to *decide* whether to train, the app tells them. It makes the app feel like a real personal trainer who knows your body. It also prevents the "overtraining spiral" that causes many people to quit — getting injured or burning out because they didn't know when to back off.

## Pillar 5 — Accountability Layer

### The Problem

Social pressure is one of the most powerful motivators in behaviour change. But current social fitness apps (Strava, Fitbit) require public profiles, follower counts, and likes — which creates comparison anxiety and performative behaviour. There's a better model: *private accountability partnerships* with one or two people you actually trust.

### What It Does

**Accountability Partners (1–3 people)**  
Invite a friend, partner, or coach by email. They don't need accounts with full access — they receive a weekly summary email with a simple view of your progress.

**Weekly Check-in Summary Email**  
Every Sunday evening, your accountability partner(s) receive a beautifully formatted email (or in-app notification if they have the app):
- Days logged this week: 6/7
- Streak: 14 days
- Protein goal hit: 5/7 days
- Workouts completed: 3
- One line from your weekly note
- A single message area for them to reply with encouragement

**In-App Partner View**  
Partners with accounts can see a lightweight dashboard of your stats (not your full log — privacy-preserving). They see the same summary data that goes in the email.

**Streak Shield from Partner**  
If you're about to break a streak, the app can optionally send your accountability partner a nudge: *"Nathan hasn't logged today — it's 9pm. Send them a message?"* Partners can tap a single-tap encouragement that sends you a push notification.

**Group Challenges (2–8 people)**  
A group of users agree on a shared challenge:
- *"All of us log every day for 30 days"*
- *"Hit protein goal 5 days this week"*
- *"Complete 3 workouts each this week"*

Each challenge has a shared leaderboard (anonymous by default, can reveal names). When anyone in the group hits a milestone, everyone gets a push notification. Challenges are opt-in and time-limited.

### New Data Model

```sql
-- Accountability relationships
CREATE TABLE accountability_partners (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  partner_email text NOT NULL,
  partner_user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id),  -- null if not signed up
  status text DEFAULT 'pending',   -- 'pending', 'active', 'declined'
  share_level text DEFAULT 'summary',  -- 'summary', 'full'
  invited_at timestamptz DEFAULT now(),
  accepted_at timestamptz,
  UNIQUE(user_id, partner_email)
);

-- Group challenges
CREATE TABLE challenges (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  creator_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id),
  name text NOT NULL,
  description text,
  challenge_type text NOT NULL,    -- 'streak', 'protein_days', 'workout_count'
  target_value int NOT NULL,
  start_date date NOT NULL,
  end_date date NOT NULL,
  is_anonymous boolean DEFAULT true,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);

CREATE TABLE challenge_members (
  challenge_id uuid REFERENCES challenges(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id),
  display_name text,               -- anonymous alias if is_anonymous
  progress int DEFAULT 0,
  joined_at timestamptz DEFAULT now(),
  PRIMARY KEY(challenge_id, user_id)
);
```

### Implementation Notes

- The weekly summary email uses Resend (or similar) with a React Email template — this is the same tech stack and is free up to 3,000 emails/month
- Partner invites work via email magic link — no password required for partners who just want to cheer you on
- Challenge progress is computed by the nightly cron job alongside insights generation
- Push notifications to partners use the same VAPID push infrastructure already built

### Why This Matters

Accountability is the single biggest predictor of goal adherence in the behaviour change literature. This feature doesn't require building a social network — it's intimate, private, and focused on support rather than performance. It also dramatically increases retention: users who have an accountability partner are far less likely to churn.

## Pillar 6 — Health Platform Integrations

### The Problem

The app currently sits in a silo. Apple Health, Google Fit, Oura, Withings, and Garmin all have richer data — especially sleep staging, HRV, step counts, and passive calorie burn — that would make every existing feature more accurate. Manual logging is effortful; automatic sync removes friction.

### What It Does

**Apple Health (iOS only)**  
Read:
- Steps (daily), active energy burned
- Heart rate, resting heart rate, HRV (if Apple Watch)
- Sleep analysis (in bed / asleep / awake stages)
- Body weight (syncs to `body_metrics` automatically)
- Workouts (syncs to `workouts` table, de-duplicated against Strava)

Write:
- Log saved workouts back to Health as workout sessions
- Log body weight entries

Implementation: Apple HealthKit API via a React Native wrapper or Next.js Web extension. For a pure PWA, this requires a native shell — either a WKWebView wrapper app submitted to the App Store, or a Capacitor/Expo build. This is the only feature that requires a native app.

**Google Fit / Health Connect (Android)**  
Same read/write scope as Apple Health. Accessible via the Health Connect Android API or Google Fit REST API. Can be implemented in a PWA via `navigator.health` (experimental) or via a Trusted Web Activity (TWA) wrapper on the Play Store.

**Oura Ring**  
Read via OAuth + REST API:
- Readiness score (from Oura's own model)
- Sleep staging (REM, deep, light, awake durations)
- HRV, SpO2, body temperature deviation

Feeds directly into Pillar 4 (Readiness Score), replacing the estimated score with Oura's gold-standard data. This is the highest-signal integration for the recovery use case.

**Withings Smart Scale**  
Read via OAuth + REST API:
- Body weight (kg, lbs, auto-converts)
- Body fat %, muscle mass, bone mass, BMI
- Syncs automatically to `body_metrics` — no manual entry required

**Implementation Priority**

| Integration | Effort | Value | Platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| Withings Smart Scale | Low (REST API) | High (weight logging is annoying manually) | Web/iOS/Android |
| Oura Ring | Medium (OAuth) | Very High (enriches readiness score) | Web |
| Google Health Connect | High (native required) | High | Android |
| Apple HealthKit | Very High (native shell) | Very High | iOS only |

Start with Withings and Oura — both are pure REST APIs accessible from the web without a native app.

### New Routes

- `GET/POST /api/integrations/withings/auth` — OAuth flow
- `POST /api/integrations/withings/sync` — pull latest weight + body comp
- `GET/POST /api/integrations/oura/auth` — OAuth flow
- `GET /api/integrations/oura/readiness` — pull readiness and sleep data

### New Data Model

```sql
-- Extend integrations table (likely already exists for Strava)
ALTER TABLE integrations ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS provider_metadata jsonb;

-- Body composition (extends body_metrics)
ALTER TABLE body_metrics
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS body_fat_pct numeric,
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS muscle_mass_kg numeric,
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS bone_mass_kg numeric,
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS source text DEFAULT 'manual';  -- 'manual', 'withings', 'apple_health'

-- Sleep staging (from Oura / Apple Health)
CREATE TABLE sleep_records (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  date date NOT NULL,
  total_sleep_min int,
  rem_sleep_min int,
  deep_sleep_min int,
  light_sleep_min int,
  awake_min int,
  sleep_score int,          -- 0-100 from provider, or null if manual
  hrv_avg numeric,
  source text NOT NULL,     -- 'oura', 'apple_health', 'manual'
  raw_data jsonb,
  UNIQUE(user_id, date, source)
);
```

### Why This Matters

Every extra data source makes the correlation engine, readiness score, and AI coaching more accurate. More importantly, *automatic sync removes the single biggest source of churn*: forgetting to log. A user whose weight syncs from their scale, whose workouts come from their watch, and whose sleep comes from their ring only needs to manually log food — cutting the daily effort by 60%.

### Idea 1 — Hydration Tracker

**The gap:** The app tracks food, macros, sleep, stress, alcohol, and energy — but not water intake. Hydration directly affects energy, cognitive performance, and workout output, and it correlates with multiple fields already being logged.

**What it would do:**
- A simple water log widget in the daily log: tap a glass/cup icon to add 250ml increments, or enter a custom amount
- Daily target (default 2.5L, adjustable; auto-bumps on workout days and in hot weather based on logged movement)
- Reminder notifications: configurable intervals (e.g. every 2h from 8am–8pm) that smart-skip if the user has already hit their daily target
- Feed water intake into the Correlation Engine as a variable: correlate with energy level, headaches, workout performance

**Why it matters:** Hydration is the easiest-to-influence health variable. Users who track water drink significantly more of it. It's a lightweight daily touch point that keeps users opening the app throughout the day (not just at mealtimes), which is good for retention.

**Effort estimate:** 1–2 days. No new AI calls; the correlation engine and reminder infrastructure are already built.

### Idea 2 — Fasting & Time-Restricted Eating Tracker

**The gap:** Intermittent fasting (IF) and time-restricted eating (TRE) are among the most popular dietary protocols. The app has no concept of eating windows — it can't tell if a user is doing 16:8, 18:6, or OMAD, and it doesn't help them track adherence.

**What it would do:**
- **Fasting timer:** A persistent card on the daily log showing "Fasting: 11h 23m / 16h" with a visual progress arc. One tap to start or break the fast.
- **Protocol presets:** 16:8, 18:6, OMAD, 5:2, custom. Stored in `user_settings`.
- **Eating window indicator:** Overlay on the daily log timeline showing the allowed eating window shaded in green.
- **Weekly adherence:** Track which days the user stayed within their window. Show adherence % in trends.
- **Smart AI integration:** The AI coach knows about fasting windows and adjusts meal suggestions and macro timing accordingly (e.g. "Your eating window opens in 2h — here's a high-protein meal to break your fast").
- **Correlation with energy/mood:** Feed fasting adherence into the Correlation Engine.

**New data model (minimal):**
```sql
ALTER TABLE user_settings
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS fasting_protocol text DEFAULT null,  -- '16:8', '18:6', 'omad', 'custom', null
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS fasting_window_hours int,            -- duration of fast
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS eating_window_start time;            -- local time eating window opens

-- Log fast events per day
ALTER TABLE daily_logs
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS fast_start_time timestamptz,
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS fast_end_time timestamptz,
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS fast_completed boolean;
```

**Why it matters:** IF is one of the top searched fitness topics globally. Supporting it would unlock a large segment of users who currently use dedicated fasting apps (Zero, Fastic) alongside a fitness tracker — there's an opportunity to consolidate.

**Effort estimate:** 2–3 days.

### Idea 3 — Injury & Soreness Map

**The gap:** The app can tell users to rest if their readiness is low, but it has no knowledge of *why* — whether it's systemic fatigue, a sore left knee, or a tweaked rotator cuff. Tracking injury and soreness prevents the app from recommending exercises that would make things worse, and provides a recovery timeline.

**What it would do:**
- **Body diagram:** An anterior/posterior body silhouette with tappable muscle groups. Users mark soreness (1–5 scale) or injury per region.
- **Logged daily:** Optional field at the bottom of the daily log ("Any soreness or pain today?"). Can be skipped.
- **Workout filtering:** When an injury is logged, the workout recommender excludes exercises that stress that region. Shown as a warning: "Avoiding chest/shoulder exercises — you logged right shoulder soreness."
- **Recovery tracking:** Each injury entry can include notes, estimated recovery date, and status (active / improving / resolved). Plot recovery duration over time.
- **Readiness integration:** Active injuries reduce the readiness score by a configurable amount (severity × weight).
- **Historical log:** A "pain journal" view showing injury history and typical recovery times, useful for spotting patterns ("you tend to strain your lower back after squat volume spikes").

**New data model:**
```sql
CREATE TABLE injury_logs (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  date date NOT NULL,
  body_region text NOT NULL,      -- 'left_shoulder', 'lower_back', 'right_knee', etc.
  severity int NOT NULL,          -- 1 (mild soreness) to 5 (acute injury)
  injury_type text,               -- 'soreness', 'strain', 'tendinitis', 'other'
  notes text,
  status text DEFAULT 'active',   -- 'active', 'improving', 'resolved'
  resolved_date date,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);
```

**Why it matters:** Injury is the most common reason people quit fitness programmes. An app that helps users manage and recover from injury, rather than ignoring it, builds trust and becomes a long-term companion rather than a fair-weather tool.

**Effort estimate:** 3–4 days (body diagram SVG is the most complex part; everything else is straightforward).

### Idea 4 — Strength Benchmarking (Age & Gender Adjusted)

**The gap:** The app shows users their lifts over time, but nothing tells them *how good those numbers are*. Is a 100kg squat impressive for a 35-year-old woman? Is a 120kg bench press average or exceptional for a 28-year-old man? Without context, raw numbers are hard to interpret.

**What it would do:**
- For each major lift (squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press, row), calculate the user's estimated 1RM (already planned in Pillar 3) and compare it against age/gender/bodyweight adjusted norms.
- Display a percentile ranking: *"Your estimated 1RM bench press (97.5kg) puts you in the 72nd percentile for men aged 30–39."*
- Show a simple bar or gauge UI on the exercise history modal.
- Optionally display these on a "Strength Profile" card on the Trends page.
- Use public strength standards (e.g. ExRx.net strength standards, which are freely available and can be hardcoded as a lookup table).
- Provide milestone labels: Untrained → Novice → Intermediate → Advanced → Elite, with the user's current position highlighted.

**Why it matters:** Percentile benchmarks are one of the most motivating UX patterns in fitness. They give users a goal ("I want to reach the 80th percentile on deadlift") and make progress legible without requiring external comparison. No social network needed.

**Effort estimate:** 1–2 days. The data is static (a lookup table of standards by gender/age/bodyweight bracket). Biggest work is calculating 1RM accurately, which is already planned in Pillar 3.

### Idea 5 — AI Coach Persona Selector

**The gap:** The AI coach currently has one voice — presumably warm and supportive. But different users want fundamentally different relationships with their coach. Some want tough love; others want data-only feedback; others want a conversational buddy. A single persona can't optimally serve all of them.

**What it would do:**
- During onboarding (or in Settings), the user picks a coaching style:
  - **Supportive** — warm, encouraging, focuses on effort over outcomes. *"You're making real progress and that takes grit."*
  - **Tough Love** — direct, holds you accountable, doesn't sugarcoat. *"You missed your protein target 4 days this week. That's not good enough — here's the plan."*
  - **Data Coach** — pure analytics, minimal emotion. *"Correlation r=0.41: protein adherence ↔ energy. Target: 145g. Yesterday: 112g. Delta: −33g."*
  - **Casual** — friend-like, uses humour, casual language. *"ok so you crushed it today ngl 💪 also maybe lay off the protein bars, they're not actually that great"*
- The persona is stored in `user_settings` and injected into every Claude system prompt as a style instruction.
- A sample message is shown when selecting a persona so users can preview the tone before committing.

**New data model:**
```sql
ALTER TABLE user_settings ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS coach_persona text DEFAULT 'supportive';
-- values: 'supportive', 'tough_love', 'data_coach', 'casual'
```

**Why it matters:** Very low implementation effort, very high perceived personalisation. Users who feel the app "gets" them are dramatically more likely to retain. It also differentiates from every other fitness app, which all use the same saccharine coaching voice.

**Effort estimate:** Half a day — it's purely a system prompt modification.

### Idea 6 — Non-Scale Victory (NSV) Tracker

**The gap:** The app heavily measures quantitative outcomes: weight, macros, workout volume, streaks. But many of the most meaningful fitness milestones are qualitative: first pull-up, running a mile without stopping, fitting into old clothes, no longer getting winded on the stairs. When the scale doesn't move, these wins keep people going — but they're invisible in the app.

**What it would do:**
- A dedicated "Wins" log section (below or alongside body metrics) for recording NSVs.
- Users type a short description and pick a category: **Strength**, **Endurance**, **Flexibility**, **Body**, **Energy/Mood**, **Habit**, **Other**.
- NSVs show in the progress timeline alongside body metrics and progress photos.
- The AI coach is aware of recent NSVs and references them: *"You hit your first pull-up last week — that reflects weeks of back work paying off."*
- Milestone NSVs can optionally trigger a confetti/badge moment in the app.
- A "Win Feed" on the progress page shows a reverse-chronological list of all logged NSVs.

**New data model:**
```sql
CREATE TABLE non_scale_victories (
  id uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid REFERENCES auth.users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  date date NOT NULL DEFAULT current_date,
  description text NOT NULL,
  category text NOT NULL,    -- 'strength', 'endurance', 'flexibility', 'body', 'energy', 'habit', 'other'
  is_milestone boolean DEFAULT false,
  created_at timestamptz DEFAULT now()
);
```

**Why it matters:** NSVs are psychologically critical for long-term adherence. Users who only measure weight often quit when they hit a plateau. Users who also measure how they *feel* and *perform* have a much richer success metric that doesn't have plateaus. This feature costs almost nothing to build and pays outsized dividends in retention.

**Effort estimate:** 1 day.

### Idea 7 — Monthly Health Digest Email

**The gap:** The app already generates weekly AI insights and has an email infrastructure (push notifications + Resend for accountability emails). But the user themselves only ever sees data when they open the app. A monthly email summary would bring disengaged users back and make active users feel proud of their progress.

**What it would do:**
- On the 1st of each month, users who opted in receive a beautifully formatted email with:

  - **CTA button:** "Log today →"
- Users can opt out in Settings.

**Why it matters:** Monthly digests are a proven retention mechanic (Spotify Wrapped is the canonical example). They make users feel proud of their data, create a shareable moment, and re-activate lapsed users. The whole feature uses existing data and the Resend/email infrastructure already planned for accountability emails.

**Effort estimate:** 2–3 days (React Email template + cron job + data aggregation query).

### Idea 8 — Pantry-Aware Meal Suggestions

**The gap:** The Nutrition page already has a pantry API endpoint. But if users log what's in their pantry, the meal planner currently ignores it. The most useful thing a meal planner can do is suggest what to cook *with what you already have*, reducing the gap between plan and execution.

**What it would do:**
- The existing pantry scan/entry stores what ingredients the user has at home.
- When generating a weekly meal plan (Pillar 2), add a mode: **"Use What I Have"** — the AI prioritises meals that can be made from pantry items, supplementing with a minimal shopping list for gaps.
- Display a traffic-light indicator on each planned meal: 🟢 Have all ingredients, 🟡 Need 1–2 items, 🔴 Need most ingredients.
- When pantry items are used in a logged meal, optionally decrement them (or prompt "Did you use the chicken breast you had?").
- The grocery list generator (already planned in Pillar 2) integrates with pantry to only list items not already in stock.

**Why it matters:** The hardest part of meal planning is the gap between plan and fridge. Pantry-aware suggestions make plans actually executable, dramatically improving adherence to the meal plan. It also differentiates from generic AI meal planners.

**Effort estimate:** 1–2 days to wire pantry data into the existing meal plan prompt (the pantry API already exists).

### Idea 9 — Adaptive Calorie Targets

**The gap:** The app sets static daily calorie and macro targets based on the Goal Wizard's initial calculation. But a user's actual energy expenditure varies significantly day to day — a rest day burns 400–600 fewer calories than a heavy training day. Eating the same target every day means overeating on rest days and under-fuelling on workout days.

**What it would do:**
- Each morning, calculate a **dynamic calorie target** for the day based on:
  - The user's base TDEE (from Goal Wizard)
  - Whether today has a scheduled workout (and its estimated intensity/duration)
  - The readiness score (low readiness = likely rest day)
  - Weekly average calorie adherence (if user is consistently hitting targets, maintain; if they're not, flag it)
- Show the adjusted target in the daily log: *"Today's target: 2,340 kcal (workout day +200)"*
- Allow the user to override with a single tap.
- Track how adaptive vs. static targets compare over time in Trends.

**New data model:** No new tables. Add a `dynamic_calorie_target` computed field to the daily log view, populated by the existing readiness score data.

**Why it matters:** Precision nutrition matters at the margins, and the existing static approach is a known inaccuracy. This makes the app's nutrition guidance materially more accurate without requiring any new data from the user — it's purely smarter use of what's already there.

**Effort estimate:** 1–2 days (logic is straightforward; UI change is minimal).

### Idea 10 — PDF Health Summary Export

**The gap:** Users accumulate months of rich health data that lives entirely inside the app. There's no way to share it with a doctor, personal trainer, or nutritionist, or to keep a personal archive outside the app. This limits the app's utility for users who want to use it as a medical or professional tool.

**What it would do:**
- A **"Generate Health Report"** button in Settings or on the Progress page.
- User selects a date range (last 30, 90, 180 days, or custom) and chooses what to include:
  - Body weight trend chart
  - Workout volume trend (by muscle group)
  - Average daily macros vs. targets
  - Sleep quality trend
  - Key personal records (strength milestones)
  - Any logged NSVs
  - Optional: a one-paragraph AI summary of the period
- Output: a clean, printable PDF (2–4 pages). Can be downloaded or shared as a link.

**Technical approach:** Use a server-side PDF generation library (Puppeteer, @react-pdf/renderer, or similar). Generate an HTML page with the charts (using the same Recharts components) and render it to PDF.

**Why it matters:** This is a feature almost no consumer fitness app offers. It positions the app as a serious health tool, not a gamified tracker. It's particularly valuable for users who work with coaches or have health conditions being monitored. It also reduces lock-in anxiety — users know they can export their data.

**Effort estimate:** 3–4 days (PDF generation + chart rendering server-side is the hard part).

### Idea 11 — Circadian Rhythm Optimisation

**The gap:** The app tracks when users exercise and eat but never tells them *when they should*. Circadian biology is increasingly mainstream — morning vs evening chronotypes have measurable effects on optimal workout timing, meal spacing, and sleep schedule. This is an untapped intelligence layer.

**What it would do:**
- **Chronotype quiz** (5 questions) at onboarding or in Settings: determines if the user is a morning type (early wake, early peak alertness), evening type (late wake, late peak), or intermediate.
- **Optimal window overlay:** Shows on the daily log calendar a shaded "peak performance window" (2–4 hours) when the user's body temperature, coordination, and strength are likely at their highest — typically 4–7 hours after wake time for most people.
- **Meal timing guide:** Based on chronotype and fasting preference, suggest optimal meal spacing (e.g. break fast within 2h of wake, last meal 3h before sleep).
- **AI-aware scheduling:** When the user asks the AI coach "When should I work out today?" — the coach knows the chronotype and suggests a specific time window.
- **Correlation tracking:** Over time, correlate actual workout time vs. readiness score the next day to personalise the recommendations based on the user's own data.

**New data model:**
```sql
ALTER TABLE user_settings ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS chronotype text DEFAULT 'intermediate';
-- values: 'morning', 'intermediate', 'evening'
ALTER TABLE user_settings ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS typical_wake_time time;
ALTER TABLE user_settings ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS typical_sleep_time time;
```

**Why it matters:** Chronobiology is a growing area of public interest, validated by peer-reviewed research. Very few apps apply it in a personalised way. It's a differentiator that makes the app feel scientifically sophisticated and gives users actionable guidance they can't get from a generic fitness tracker.

**Effort estimate:** 2–3 days (quiz + overlay UI is simple; the AI integration is a system prompt addition).

### Idea 12 — AI Post-Workout Summary Card

### Idea 13 — Supplement Tracker

**The gap:** Many fitness-focused users take supplements (creatine, protein powder, pre-workout, omega-3, vitamin D, magnesium, etc.) and want to track them reliably — both for adherence and for correlating with performance. **What it would do:**

- **Adherence tracking:** Trends view shows adherence % by supplement over 30/90 days.
- **Correlation integration:** Feed supplement adherence into the Correlation Engine. *"Your energy is 28% higher on days you take your vitamin D."*

CREATE TABLE user_supplements (

### Idea 14 — Workout Difficulty Auto-Rating

**The gap:** The app logs what you did in a workout but doesn't capture how hard it felt. Perceived exertion (RPE — Rate of Perceived Exertion) is one of the most useful signals for training load management, and it's the missing link between volume data and recovery data.

**What it would do:**
- At the end of each workout, a single quick prompt: *"How hard was that? (1–10)"* — a tap on a large, friendly slider.
- Optional second prompt: *"How do you feel right now?"* (energised / normal / tired / exhausted) — four buttons.
- These two fields are stored on the `workouts` record.
- RPE feeds directly into the Readiness Score algorithm (high RPE yesterday → lower readiness today).
- Over time, correlate RPE vs. next-day energy, next-day sleep, and actual performance in the following workout.
- Show average RPE trend in the Volume Tracking Dashboard (Pillar 3) — a rising trend without improving lifts = overreaching signal.

**New data model:**
```sql
ALTER TABLE workouts
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS rpe int,             -- 1-10, subjective effort
  ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS post_feeling text;   -- 'energised', 'normal', 'tired', 'exhausted'
```

**Why it matters:** RPE is used by elite athletes and coaches worldwide as the most practical training load metric. It takes 3 seconds to capture and dramatically improves the accuracy of the readiness score, overtraining detection, and training program progression. It's the simplest possible data collection with the highest return.

**Effort estimate:** Half a day — a small modal after workout save, two fields to store.
